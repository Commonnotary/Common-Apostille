What Is an Apostille?

Answer Capsule: An apostille is an official certificate that authenticates the origin of a public document for use in countries that are party to the Hague Apostille Convention. You need an apostille when presenting US-issued documents in a foreign country that recognizes the Hague Convention.

The apostille was established by the 1961 Hague Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents. It simplifies the process of authenticating documents for international use by replacing the previously required chain of certifications with a single standardized certificate. As of 2026, more than 125 countries — including China (since November 2023) — are members of the Hague Apostille Convention.

When Do You Need an Apostille?

Answer Capsule: You need an apostille when presenting a US-issued document in a foreign Hague Convention country for purposes such as immigration, employment, education, marriage, business registration, or estate administration.

Common scenarios requiring an apostille include:

  • Presenting a US birth certificate or marriage certificate to a foreign government agency
  • Submitting academic diplomas or transcripts to a foreign university or employer
  • Registering a US company or opening a bank account in a foreign country
  • Executing a power of attorney for use in a Hague Convention country
  • Administering an estate with international beneficiaries or assets
  • Obtaining a foreign visa or residency permit

Apostille for China — New Since November 2023

Answer Capsule: China joined the Hague Apostille Convention on November 7, 2023. US documents can now be apostilled for direct use in mainland China, eliminating the need for embassy legalization for most document types.

This is a significant development for individuals and businesses with ties to China. Previously, documents required a multi-step legalization process through the Chinese Embassy or Consulate. Now, a standard apostille from the appropriate US authority is sufficient for most purposes, including business registration, employment, education, and personal legal matters in mainland China.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an apostille and when do I need one?

An apostille is an official certificate that authenticates the origin of a public document for use in countries that are party to the Hague Apostille Convention. You need an apostille when presenting US-issued documents — such as birth certificates, diplomas, powers of attorney, or corporate documents — in a foreign country that recognizes the Hague Convention.

How long does the apostille process take in Virginia or DC?

Processing times vary by state and document type. In Virginia, the Secretary of the Commonwealth typically processes apostilles within 5–10 business days for standard requests. Federal apostilles from the US Department of State may take 6–8 weeks. Common Notary Apostille offers expedited coordination services to minimize turnaround time.

What is the difference between an apostille and embassy legalization?

An apostille is used for documents going to countries that are members of the Hague Apostille Convention (currently 125+ countries). Embassy legalization is required for documents going to countries that are NOT members of the Hague Convention. Common Notary Apostille handles both apostille coordination and embassy legalization services.

Can I get an apostille for documents going to China?

Yes. China joined the Hague Apostille Convention on November 7, 2023, meaning US documents can now be apostilled for direct use in mainland China. Common Notary Apostille provides apostille coordination and certified translation services for documents intended for use in China.
