Apostille Services

What is an apostille and when do I need one?

An apostille is an official certificate that authenticates the origin of a public document for use in countries that are party to the Hague Apostille Convention. You need an apostille when presenting US-issued documents — such as birth certificates, diplomas, powers of attorney, or corporate documents — in a foreign country that recognizes the Hague Convention. Common Notary Apostille coordinates the complete apostille process for documents originating in Virginia, Maryland, and Washington DC.

How long does the apostille process take in Virginia or DC?

Processing times vary by state and document type. In Virginia, the Secretary of the Commonwealth typically processes apostilles within 5–10 business days for standard requests. Federal apostilles from the US Department of State may take 6–8 weeks. Common Notary Apostille offers expedited coordination services to minimize turnaround time.

What is the difference between an apostille and embassy legalization?

An apostille is used for documents going to countries that are members of the Hague Apostille Convention (currently 125+ countries). Embassy legalization (also called consular legalization or authentication) is required for documents going to countries that are NOT members of the Hague Convention. Common Notary Apostille handles both apostille coordination and embassy legalization services.

Can I get an apostille for documents going to China?

Yes. China joined the Hague Apostille Convention on November 7, 2023, meaning US documents can now be apostilled for direct use in mainland China. Common Notary Apostille provides apostille coordination and certified translation services for documents intended for use in China, including corporate documents, personal records, and legal instruments. Learn more about our China apostille services.

What documents can be apostilled?

Common documents that can be apostilled include birth certificates, marriage certificates, death certificates, diplomas and transcripts, powers of attorney, corporate documents (articles of incorporation, certificates of good standing), court documents, and notarized documents. The document must first be notarized or issued by a government authority before an apostille can be attached.

What are the legal requirements for notarizing a will in Washington DC?

In Washington DC, a valid will must be signed by the testator in the presence of two competent witnesses who are not beneficiaries. While DC does not require notarization of the will itself, a self-proving affidavit — which is notarized — allows the will to be admitted to probate without requiring witness testimony. Common Notary Apostille facilitates the complete execution process, including coordinating witnesses and notarizing the self-proving affidavit in compliance with DC Code § 18-103.
